# Web3 KOL spotlight: Vitalik Buterin — reframing what ETH is actually worth

*2026-11-27 · post 3 of 3*

Today's spotlight is **Vitalik Buterin** — [@VitalikButerin](https://x.com/VitalikButerin) on X, co-founder of Ethereum.

Why him, why now: in the last three weeks Vitalik has publicly rethought how Ethereum itself should be valued — and defended a controversial upgrade in the same breath. Two things worth knowing:

**1. ETH as a store of value, not a fee machine.** In a thread posted on X on **8 November 2026**, Vitalik argued ETH should be valued as a store of value rather than on the fees the network burns. His point: Ethereum spent years cutting fees for users, so fee-burning was never going to be the thing that makes ETH valuable. Instead he points to a set of properties he calls **CROPS** — censorship and capture resistance, open-source code, privacy, and security — plus Ethereum hosting tokenized real-world assets (about $15.1 billion of them, and about $136 billion in stablecoins, as of mid-November). The takeaway for builders: value comes from what the chain can hold and protect, not from how much it charges to use it.

**2. Defending the gas overhaul.** On **16 November 2026**, Vitalik defended **EIP-8037**, part of the upcoming Glamsterdam upgrade, which would raise gas costs for creating new accounts, storage slots and smart contracts — an account would cost about 197,900 gas to create, up from 36,600 — to slow Ethereum's state growth (about 110 GiB a year). He also suggested cheaper paths for airdrops: Merkle-tree distributions, and longer-term, UTXOs with keyed nonces instead of storage slots. The upgrade itself hit a delay after researchers found contract risks, and developers are still weighing fixes.

Why this matters to me as a builder: the person who co-founded Ethereum is publicly saying the chain's value is in its properties, not its fees — and at the same time pushing changes that make building on it more expensive in the short term. Both directions point the same way: build things that are actually useful and hard to censor, because that's the ground the ecosystem is choosing to stand on.

One honest note: ETH is about 63% below its all-time high as of mid-November, and the market hasn't rewarded this reframing yet. Ideas move slower than prices — and prices move slower than ideas deserve, sometimes.
