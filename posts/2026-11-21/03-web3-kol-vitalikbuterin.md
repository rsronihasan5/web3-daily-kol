# Web3 KOL spotlight: Vitalik Buterin (@VitalikButerin)

*2026-11-21 · post 3 of 3*

**Who:** Vitalik Buterin, co-founder of Ethereum — [@VitalikButerin](https://x.com/VitalikButerin) on X.

**Why him today:** In the middle of a brutal week for ETH — down ~16% in 7 days, trading near $1,620 — Vitalik has spent November reframing what Ethereum is actually for. Two posts, two weeks apart, and both matter for anyone building on the chain.

**1. ETH as a store of value, not a fee-burning token (8 November)**

In a long X thread on 8 November, Vitalik argued that ETH's value should come from Ethereum's decentralisation, security and censorship resistance — not from how much transaction fee gets burned. His point: fee burning was never as strong a value story as it looked. The numbers back the tension: over the 30 days to 8 November, Ethereum issued about 89,614 ETH and burned only about 2,890 — roughly 3% of issuance.

He coined the shorthand **CROPS** for what should matter instead: censorship and capture resistance, open-source code, privacy, and security. His argument is that those properties make ETH a store of value in its own right, and that chasing fee revenue could actually undermine them.

**2. Going "total bull" on quantum-safe Ethereum (16 November)**

On 16 November, Vitalik threw his weight behind **leanPCS**, a technique to make Ethereum resistant to future quantum computers without forcing users to store huge quantum-safe signatures onchain. His draft proposal, **EIP-8288**, would bundle many large signatures and proofs into one compact proof before they ever reach a block. His own words: "Over the past few weeks I've gone from thinking leanPCS is total bull to being a total bull."

The proposal is still a draft with no launch date — but the direction is clear. Ethereum's researchers are now prioritising quantum defences over faster blocks in the planned 2027 upgrade, and the Ethereum Foundation formed a dedicated post-quantum team back in January.

**Why I pay attention:**

Most KOLs in this space post price calls. Vitalik posts protocol design, and then ships the reasoning in public — even when it's unpopular with people who bought ETH for the burn narrative. In a week when ETH is down hard, the co-founder isn't tweeting about the price. He's tweeting about what the chain should be worth in ten years.

That's the difference between a KOL and a builder with a following. Follow the builders.
