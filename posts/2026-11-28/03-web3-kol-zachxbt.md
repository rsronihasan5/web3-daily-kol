Today's spotlight is **ZachXBT** — [@zachxbt](https://x.com/zachxbt) on X, the independent on-chain investigator who has become crypto's unofficial detective.

Why him, why now: in November 2026 he traced one of the biggest suspected thefts of the year — and accused a privacy tool of laundering for North Korea's Lazarus Group, all within the same month.

**1. The suspected $133 million EU theft.** On **2 November 2026**, ZachXBT published an alert that an unidentified European entity may have lost more than **$106 million** from accounts at several centralized exchanges the previous day — mostly USDC and USDT, withdrawn into a cluster of external wallets and then moved through instant exchanges, bridges and Monero. On **3 November**, he raised the estimate above **$133 million** after finding another $27 million in withdrawals from the same suspected actor — some of them made *after* his first public warning. That evening he named French cooperative bank **Crédit Mutuel Arkéa** as the alleged victim. Important to be honest here: the bank has not confirmed anything, and these are ZachXBT's findings, not a verified loss. But he also said he had a high degree of confidence the attacker was **not** Lazarus Group — the laundering patterns didn't match.

**2. Accusing a CoinJoin operator of laundering for Lazarus.** On **17 November 2026**, ZachXBT accused CoinJoin operator **Kruw** of helping the Lazarus Group launder stolen crypto, alleging the operator knowingly facilitated laundering tied to hundreds of millions of dollars across several exploits. None of this has been proven in court — these are allegations from an independent investigator — but his past work has led to concrete action before, including Tether freezing about 442,000 USDT after one of his earlier investigations.
