# Web3 KOL: Anatoly Yakovenko (@toly) — building the case that SOL is money

*2026-11-24 · post 3 of 3*

**Anatoly Yakovenko** — [@toly](https://x.com/toly) on X — co-founder of Solana. While the market bleeds, he's spent November doing what he always does: posting big ideas in public and letting the timeline argue about them.

Three verified recent facts:

- **18 November:** Toly pitched an **onchain basket that tracks the entire global economy** — the Magnificent Seven, SpaceX, and Chinese robotics maker Unitree in one structured product, with isolated risk per component, fully smart-contract automated, globally available, and cheaper than TradFi equivalents. His framing: "What is a sov money one could actually shill to normies?" Securitize already offers tokenized SpaceX and Mag 7 exposure on Solana for eligible investors, so the pieces of his idea are partly live already.
- **15 November:** After Firedancer developers ran a **Solana mainnet node on a tablet** — less than one CPU core, over 5G, catching up to the chain tip and voting in about 10 seconds — Toly replied to Bankless co-founder David Hoffman with two words that settled a debate from 2023: **"We did it… sol is money."** (Production validators still need far more — Anza recommends at least 12 cores and 256GB of RAM — but as a demo of how light a client can run, it landed.)
- **4 November:** One line, no context: **"If you are in data centers, pivot to crypto."** Cointelegraph ran it as breaking news the same day.

And one more from the same week: at Solana Breakpoint 2026 in London (15–17 November), Toly lost the main-stage **"Is SOL money?"** debate to Helius CEO Mert Mumtaz by community vote — then kept arguing the point on X anyway.

What I take from it as a builder:

1. **Ship the argument in public.** Toly doesn't wait for a whitepaper — he posts the idea, takes the criticism, and iterates in the open. That's the builder version of marketing.
2. **Demos beat debates.** Four years of arguing about hardware requirements ended the day a validator ran on a tablet. If you can show it, you don't have to argue it.
3. **Losing a debate is not losing the point.** He lost the vote and kept making the case. Conviction that survives a public loss is worth more than a win nobody remembers.

Follow him for the unfiltered Solana view of where this industry is going — just remember the tablet demo was a proof of concept, not a production recommendation.
